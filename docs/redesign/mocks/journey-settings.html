Settings, account and system states

Every screen and state in this journey, built live in the chosen system, with three coded options for the three open decisions. Phones are interactive: use the state chips above each one, tap through, and swipe from a phone's left edge to go back. Data is realistic sample data; prices are sample store strings (the app shows whatever the App Store or Google Play returns).

Journey summary

The settings journey is rare but high-trust: people come here to change how FIQR treats them, to pay, or to leave. Each of those has to be fast, legible and honest.

Tune FIQRReminders, check-in day, coaching style, rest timer, Health sync.
Keep my numbers rightWeight, goal and daily targets on Profile.
Pay or stop payingSee Plus, its real price and terms; subscribe, restore, manage.
Control my dataAI processing, download, privacy, delete account, support.
  • Signed decisions kept: glass only on controls (C1): headers, menus, sheets, toasts, band, tab bar; content cards stay solid white. Orb is ink (Q4). Pushed screens use a centred title and a round glass back (Q19). Destructive and confirm actions are ink (D2 convention). Switches are ink when on, never orange.
  • Units: the brief lists a units setting, but the app and backend have none (kg and cm only; no column in settings). Adding one is a new backend field, ruled out by Q6, so the phones show units in the Profile group title ("Body (kg, cm)") and nothing pretends to switch. Flag for the owner if imperial is wanted.
  • Paywall benefits are still placeholders in the code (SOW 4.6: the client has not decided what sits behind Plus). All three paywall options keep the same four lines so they compare fairly.
  • Reminder time is really "earliest nudge": the backend only sends rule-based nudges after this time (check-in ready, inactive 3 days, plateau, program complete). The recommended Settings shows a real nudge preview from those rules instead of a vague "reminder".

Decision A: the paywall

How value and price are shown honestly in Spatial Depth. Honesty checks applied to every option: the full price and period on the button, the renewal date and amount before the tap, the per-month figure only as a derived line (never as the headline price), Restore and terms always visible, no fake urgency, no pre-ticked trial.

Recommendation: Plain ledger. It is the only option where both prices are readable without a tap, and the price people pay is the largest number on the screen. Depth shows up where it helps (the chosen plan lifts forward, the screen behind steps back under the App Store sheet) rather than as the hero.

Plain ledger

Recommended

Both prices side by side, the yearly per-month figure worked out from the store price, the renewal date and amount right above the button, and the full price on the button itself. The selected plan lifts forward a few points. Least spectacle, most honest.

About 20 rows across 8 groups. The jobs that matter most are reminders, coaching style and keeping targets right; the rest is reached rarely.

Recommendation: Grouped list. It matches the iOS idiom, keeps one save model (every control saves itself), and its glass menus show what each choice produces (coaching samples, next check-in date, a real nudge preview). Profile-led wins one job by one tap; if timed tests show people edit targets often, its stat sheet can be lifted into the Grouped list's profile card later at low cost.

Recommended

The iOS idiom people already know: one profile card, then short labelled groups that each save on change. Least to learn; the long tail sits a scroll away.
