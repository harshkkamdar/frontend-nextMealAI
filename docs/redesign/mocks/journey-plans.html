Weekly check-in and plans

Every screen of the check-in and the plans area, designed in the Spatial Depth system: pages open with the screen behind stepping back, sheets float forward, glass sits only on the controls. Each phone is live. Use the chips above a phone to switch its state, tap through it, drag from its left edge to go back. Three decisions are open; each has three coded options, measurements and a recommendation. Pick one per decision; the tray at the bottom collects your picks.

How the numbers under each phone were made: phones are built at a real iPhone's 390 x 844 pt with the sizing spec, then scaled to fit. Tap targets and orange actions are counted live from the phone's top layer in the current state. Frame gaps are the longest gap between animation frames during a push, a 700 ms scroll and a sheet opening, measured in headless Chrome (Playwright), median of 5 runs with the worst run in brackets; 16.8 ms is one frame at 60 Hz. The machine was shared with other jobs, so a single worst-run spike is noise until it repeats on a device. Times are estimates: 1.2 s per tap, 0.3 s per typed character, 2 s to read each screen.

The journey

Once a week Geo reads the week and may move the daily targets. In between, people look up the plan they follow and now and then build or change one. Three jobs carry this journey:

Decide on this week's targets

Open the check-in from the Home card, see what changes and why, apply or keep. Weekly, about 2 minutes, from a push or the Home card.

Look up my plan

See today's targets, what to eat on a day, or the next workout. A few times a week, from Settings, Train or a Geo reply.

Build or change a plan

By hand, one question per step, or by asking Geo. A few times a month at most; most plans come from Geo.

What Apply really changes, read from the backend

Read-only from lib/check-in-engine.ts, services/check-in-status.service.ts and services/check-in.service.ts. The engine, not the model, makes the decision; Geo only writes the sentence. This is what a trustworthy screen has to show, and four places where today's screen and backend disagree.

How the number is made

  • The week counts only with at least 4 days of food logs and 3 weigh-ins (the screen asks for 5 logged days), and nutrition adherence of 70% or more. Otherwise targets hold.
  • Progress is the weekly weight change against the goal (for example 0.5 kg a week down); within 0.2 kg is on track.
  • A change needs two off-target weeks in a row and 2 or more weeks on the current targets. Exception: losing more than twice the goal acts at once.
  • Fat loss moves calories 7.5% (down if behind, up if too fast); muscle gain 7.5% up or 5% down. Capped at 10%, rounded to 25 kcal, never below the estimated BMR (then it holds and suggests a diet break).
  • Low mood or energy holds calories; two weeks of it suggests a deload.

What Apply writes

  • Calories to the new number. Protein stays, unless it was under its floor.
  • Fat to 28% of calories, carbs take the rest, so carbs and fat change too. Today's screen shows only calories and protein.
  • The profile targets, and the active meal plan's daily targets in place, so the plan view shows the new numbers.
  • Target history from today, so earlier days keep their old target; the Home check-in cache is cleared.
  • A row in check_ins with the decision.

Mismatches to fix before trusting any design

  1. The status endpoint builds the proposal with decideCheckIn(gathered.input), without the previous week's state, so the two-week rule can never pass and the card usually says "hold". The verdict sentence comes from a second run that has it, so the sentence can announce a change the card does not show.
  2. That second run (runWeeklyCheckIn(..., {apply:false})) writes a check_ins row on every uncached read. Apply then reads that row as "last week", so the two-week rule can pass on one week of data, and the screen state flips to mid-week once the 6-hour cache expires.
  3. "Keep current targets" on a hold week calls the same Apply endpoint. If the server-side decision differs from the card (point 1), keeping can change targets.
  4. Mid-week, "This week so far" shows the last completed week (the calendar's week ends on the last check-in day), and "3 of 4 days logged" mixes last week's count with this week's days.

Not fixed here (read-only brief, Q6 keeps data flow as is). Every option below assumes the status returns the decision Apply will make, with all four macros. Without that, no layout can be trusted.

Open decision A: how the check-in shows what changes

The screen's one question: "Do my targets change this week, and can I trust it?" Every option shows all four targets (the backend changes carbs and fat too), names where the change lands, and keeps exactly one orange action. They differ in how much the user sees before tapping.

Check-in, the other states (one design)

Mid-week, too few days, loading, load failed and offline, in the recommended ledger style. None of them has an orange action: nothing is being decided.

Open decision B: the plan overview card on Plans

The screen's one question: "What am I following right now?" Tap a card to open the plan view; it opens with the list stepping back.

Plan views (one design each)

The nutrition view answers "what do I eat on a day"; the training view answers "what is next, and start it". The training view now marks the plan's next day, which the backend already tracks (current_position).

Open decision C: the builder step pattern

Shown on the nutrition builder, the longer of the two. Tap Continue to walk the steps, or jump with the chips. The question per step stays as today: name, daily targets, a day of eating.

Training builder and edit (recommended pattern)

Both use the recommended focused-step pattern so the three builders and onboarding share one way of asking.
